Karhu Player · in development · not released

Karhu Player.

A media player for people who need to see what is really in a file. It plays a file or a stream and tells you what is in it: the frame you are on and its SMPTE timecode, the loudness of what you hear by EBU R128, every audio track and every channel in it, the subtitles and captions it carries, the decoded value of any pixel of a paused picture, and what the file says about itself against what it actually does. It is made by the people who make Rytmi, it runs on Linux and Windows, and it will be free of charge.

Made for checking broadcast files. Its loudness meter measures to EBU R128 and ITU-R BS.1770-4, its captions are CEA-608 as CTA-608-E defines them, and its timecode is the file’s own. How the loudness meter and the captions are tested, and what is not tested, is set out below, and in the manual that comes with the player in its package.

It is not released: there is no download and no date. This page says what it does today, what it plays, what we have measured, and what we do not claim, so that when it is released it can be checked against what was said here. Everything on this page is as of 10 Nov 2026 unless it carries another date.

What it does

As of 10 Nov 2026, in the build we test.

  • Frame-accurate transport. Step one frame back or forward, seek to a frame, a timecode or a time, and land on exactly that frame, B-frames and long GOPs included, in MP4, Matroska and HLS. A plain MPEG-TS file has no index to seek by: a seek in one lands on the first keyframe after the frame asked for. SMPTE timecode is read from the file, drop-frame included. A file opens paused on its first picture; play, pause, an A–B loop, and J-K-L shuttle, forward only, from a quarter speed to eight times, with sound kept at its own pitch from half speed to double.
  • Loudness by EBU R128, in EBU Mode. Momentary and short-term loudness with their maxima, integrated loudness, the loudness range of EBU Tech 3342 and true peak, of what is heard, on either of the two scales EBU Tech 3341 sets for an EBU Mode meter; each channel weighted by its position, from the stream’s own layout. Since 10 Nov 2026, the phase correlation of a stereo pair. Separately, a measurement of the whole file from start to end, of the track or pair heard, with an AC-3 track’s declared loudness (its dialnorm) beside it and a difference of more than 2 LU said.
  • Every audio track. Every track metered at once, each channel’s peak and true peak, while one is heard; a channel silent through what was read is said. Choose the track heard, or hear an MXF master’s mono tracks two at a time as stereo pairs, paired by their places (the labels a wrapper may carry are not read). Since 10 Nov 2026, send any channel of the track heard to the left or the right, hear one alone, mute one, or hear left and right as mono, without changing what is measured. A source with more channels than the speakers is folded to them by ITU-R BS.775, and the stream panel says so.
  • Subtitles and captions. Text subtitles (SRT, WebVTT, MP4 text, and ASS and SSA as plain text) and the CEA-608 captions carried in the video (H.264, HEVC and MPEG-2), each of its four channels on its own; from a local file they are read on a timeline of their own, so a jump to any frame shows what is on screen there. Since 10 Nov 2026, teletext subtitles from a local file, read by a decoder of our own: page 888 of an MXF’s OP-47 track (SMPTE 436M), and from a transport stream’s DVB teletext the page its descriptor names, or else 888. Bitmap subtitles (DVB, Blu-ray, DVD) and CEA-708 captions are named and not shown.
  • The picture as decoded. Paused, the Y′CbCr and RGB values of the pixel under the cursor; and any frame saved as a PNG. Each frame is drawn with the colour matrix its file declares; BT.2020 and P3 standard-dynamic-range pictures are converted to BT.709’s gamut, and the stream panel says when they were.
  • What the file says against what it does. Codec, size, pixel format, colour tags and frame rate, with the frame rate the file declares checked against the one it actually runs at. For MXF, what the wrapper says (its operational pattern, field order and audio track map) is read and shown, and its field order is checked against the picture inside it.
  • What happened as it played, and why. Each event numbered, with its reason and the event that led to it: a jump in the timestamps or the timecode, a picture or a block of sound the decoder reports damaged, frames dropped with when and how late, a picture that stopped changing while the clock ran, a switch from hardware to software decoding. The record exports as JSON.
  • Sound held to its picture, with a trim to move it earlier or later, and a readout of how closely it is following. The readout measures the player’s own tracking; it cannot see the delay a display or a headset adds, which is why we measure that on the screen (below).
  • Output to a chosen screen. The picture can be sent full screen to a named display. The main display is left out of that list, so the player cannot cover the screen you are working on unless you ask it to.
  • Network streams. An MPEG-TS multicast over UDP (since 8 Nov 2026), HLS on demand and live, or a plain http or https file as it downloads, opened from the command line or from a field in the window.
  • Hardware decoding where the graphics hardware offers it: D3D11VA on Windows, VA-API on Linux, and on Linux NVIDIA’s NVDEC where no VA-API driver opens, built on 10 Nov 2026 and not run on an NVIDIA graphics card. Software decoding where it does not, and by choice: a hardware decoder reports far less damage than a software one, so for checking a file for damage the player can be kept off the graphics hardware. Most hardware-decoded frames are drawn without being copied back to the processor: on Linux with Intel graphics, and on Windows since 26 Oct 2026, where each frame is copied once within the graphics processor (seen on Intel graphics, and on AMD graphics since 5 Nov 2026). Some are still copied back: on Linux HEVC 4:2:2, a layout the graphics driver does not offer and every NVDEC frame, and on Windows a frame of an odd size.
  • HEVC on the graphics hardware only, never in software. Since 10 Nov 2026: one of HEVC’s patent pools told us in November 2026 that a program decoding HEVC itself is licensed per copy, a free one included. On a computer with no graphics decoder an HEVC file does not open, and the player says why; nor does a file whose HEVC stream the graphics processor does not take (10-bit 4:2:2 on many graphics cards, for one, or monochrome on any), a local file refused as it opens with the stream’s format named. Software decoding cannot be chosen for HEVC, so what the player says of damage in an HEVC file is what the hardware decoder says.

What it plays

As of 10 Nov 2026. Every codec below is compiled into our build and checked to be there; H.264, HEVC, AV1, MPEG-2, ProRes, DNxHD and DNxHR, v210, MPEG-4 Part 2, VP8, AAC, Opus and PCM are also played by tests of ours, and since 6 and 9 Nov 2026 MP2, AC-3, DTS’s core, TrueHD and SMPTE 302M.

Containers MP4 and MOV, Matroska and WebM, MXF, MPEG-TS; HLS streams, on demand and live
Sources Local files; an MPEG-TS multicast over UDP; HLS; a plain http or https file as it downloads. Not MPEG-TS over RTP (SMPTE ST 2022-2), SRT, RTMP, RTSP, MPEG-DASH or NDI
Video H.264, H.265/HEVC (on the graphics hardware only, never in software), VP9 and AV1; MPEG-2 (its 4:2:2 profile included), ProRes 422, DNxHD and DNxHR, and v210; MPEG-4 Part 2, H.263 and VP8
Pictures shown Planar 4:2:0 and 4:2:2 at 8 and 10 bits, and packed 8-bit 4:2:2 (uncompressed 2vuy), limited or full range. Planar 4:2:2 since 25 Oct 2026, packed since 26 Oct 2026; drawn as it is coded, each line with its own colour
Audio AAC, MP3, MP2, AC-3 (as mastered, with no dynamic range compression), FLAC, Opus and Vorbis; PCM in every common form, and SMPTE 302M; DTS’s core (a DTS-HD Master Audio track plays its core, a DTS Express track does not decode); Dolby TrueHD. In a file with a picture, an audio file’s cover art included. Not decoded: E-AC-3 (Dolby Digital Plus). Dolby E, and anything else carried in a PCM track as SMPTE 337 data, is named and muted, never played or measured as the noise it is
Subtitles Shown: SRT, WebVTT, MP4 text, ASS and SSA (as plain text), CEA-608 captions in the video, and teletext subtitles (OP-47 in MXF, DVB teletext in MPEG-TS) from a local file. Named and not shown: DVB, Blu-ray and DVD subtitles, and CEA-708 captions

Its media libraries are FFmpeg’s, built by us from FFmpeg’s own source under the GNU Lesser General Public License (LGPL) version 2.1, with nothing licensed under the GPL inside them. Their source and the script that built them will be published beside each download. Why we build them ourselves is in the archive: why we build our own FFmpeg. On Linux the download also carries libva, the runtime of VA-API, built by us from Intel’s release, under the MIT licence.

Where it is tested

As of 10 Nov 2026, on three machines: two with Intel graphics, and one with AMD graphics since 5 Nov 2026. Until 7 Oct 2026 it was developed on a laptop with AMD Radeon Vega graphics under Linux.

  • A laptop, a Lenovo ThinkPad E16 Gen 3 with an Intel Core Ultra 7 255H, under Ubuntu 24.04 LTS: where it is developed, and where every change is tested.
  • An Intel mini PC, a GMKtec NucBox G3 with an Intel N100, under Windows 11 and under Ubuntu 24.04 LTS: where the Windows build is tested, and where the sound and picture above were measured.
  • An AMD mini PC, a GMKtec M6 Ultra with an AMD Ryzen 5 7640HS and Radeon 760M graphics, under Windows 11 since 5 Nov 2026, and since 9 Nov 2026 under Ubuntu 24.04 LTS as its installer sets it up by default, with no VA-API driver: there it decodes in software.

The mini PCs are tested on ordinary computer monitors, 4K among them.

What we don’t claim

As of 10 Nov 2026
  • Not tested on NVIDIA graphics. It is tested on Intel graphics under Linux and Windows, and on AMD graphics under Windows since 5 Nov 2026. Under Linux it has run on AMD graphics only in software decoding since 7 Oct 2026. It has never run on NVIDIA graphics: on Linux its NVIDIA decoding path is built and has not run on an NVIDIA card.
  • What it says about damage is what the decoder says. In the files we damaged ourselves (6 Nov 2026, above), 28 of the 74 faults that showed went unreported in software decoding. No graphics hardware decoder we have tried flags a damaged picture, and the AMD mini PC’s held the last good picture for two seconds with nothing reported. The player says when the picture has stopped changing (a hold of 1.6 seconds or more is always said, one under a second never), which caught that hold; for checking a file for damage, software decoding is the better choice, and the player offers it.
  • 4K broadcast mezzanines decoded in software do not all keep their rate on a small computer. On the Intel mini PC, decoding alone, 4K 59.94 ProRes HQ, DNxHR HQX and 4:2:2 H.264 reached at most 62 % of their rate (30 Oct 2026). On the AMD mini PC, 4K 59.94 ProRes HQ and DNxHR HQX played at 58–60 frames a second, dropping about 1–2 % of their frames (5 Nov 2026).
  • No HEVC without a graphics decoder for it. Since 10 Nov 2026 HEVC is decoded on the graphics hardware only: a computer without a decoder for the file’s HEVC does not open it, and software decoding, the better choice for checking a file for damage, cannot be chosen for HEVC.
  • On 24 Oct 2026 the sound reached the listener 10 ms (Ubuntu) and 22 ms (Windows) after its picture on the set-up we measured, by latency that no device reported to us. The Windows build has changed how it draws since then. It has been measured once since, on 7 Nov 2026, on another machine, display and sound path, not comparable with these figures. We have not corrected for it, and on 24 Oct we measured one display and one headset.
  • Under GNOME the player drops one picture every ten to fifteen seconds, and it is the desktop’s. On 3 Nov 2026 we traced it, on two machines, to GNOME Shell’s JavaScript garbage collector running across the compositor’s own deadline: the desktop shows one picture for a second refresh, and the player skips one to catch up and logs it. The trace, and what the player does and does not do about a miss it cannot prevent, is in the archive.
  • Captions and teletext are tested on files we made. The captions’ text and times are checked against what the bytes say, through FFmpeg’s own CEA-608 decoder, in each of 608’s modes and on each of its four channels; the teletext on files of our own making. No captioned broadcast file, no real OP-47 file and no broadcast transport stream’s teletext has been read. Teletext has been read on a screen once, on 10 Nov 2026.
  • Seeking in MXF is not covered by our frame-accuracy test.
  • Not every picture is shown. 4:4:4 (ProRes 4444, DNxHR 444), 12-bit, RGB, and semi-planar 4:2:2 pictures (NV16, P210) are not drawn: the file plays, with its sound, timecode and loudness, and the picture area names the format it could not show. HDR (PQ and HLG) is not tone mapped, and the stream panel says so. Interlaced video is shown as it is coded, both fields in one frame, not deinterlaced.
  • The Windows program is not code-signed. Windows’ Smart App Control judges an unsigned program file by file. On our Intel mini PC it refused one of our test programs on 25 Oct 2026 and allowed it five minutes later, and it refused our unsigned installer on 27 Oct and on 9 Nov 2026, the second time installing the same file seven minutes later.
  • A graphics driver reset, survived once. On 3 Nov 2026, on the Intel mini PC under Windows, a driver reset forced with Microsoft’s own tool during a 4K HEVC file decoded on the graphics hardware: a development build of 30 Oct 2026 drew its picture again, opened the file again where it was and played on. The picture was gone for about 4.5 seconds after the loss, and frames were dropped in the graphics stall before it. On Linux it has survived only a graphics device destroyed on purpose; a real driver reset there has not been tried. The build we test has not been through a real driver reset, and by our reading of its code, if no graphics decoder is there when it opens the file again, an HEVC file is not opened again and its picture stays as the loss left it to the end, its sound playing on, where another codec comes back decoded in software.
  • Sound on Windows after its audio service restarts. If Windows’ audio service is restarted while the player runs, the player stays silent until it is started again.
  • No macOS, and on Linux the native Wayland window is not measured: every measurement on Linux has been taken through X11 on Xwayland.
  • Computer displays only. The picture goes to the computer’s own displays: there is no SDI or IP output, and AFD is not shown.
