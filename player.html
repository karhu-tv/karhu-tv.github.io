Karhu Player · in development · not released

Karhu Player.

A media player for people who need to see what is really in a file. It plays a file or a stream and tells you what is in it: the frame you are on and its SMPTE timecode, the loudness of what you hear by EBU R128, the decoded value of any pixel of a paused picture, and what the file says about itself against what it actually does. It is made by the people who make Rytmi, it runs on Linux and Windows, and it will be free of charge.

It is not released: there is no download and no date. This page says what it does today, what it plays, what we have measured, and what we do not claim, so that when it is released it can be checked against what was said here. Everything on this page is as of 25 Oct 2026 unless it carries another date.

What it does

As of 29 Oct 2026, in the build we test.

  • Frame-accurate transport. Step one frame back or forward, seek to a frame, a timecode or a time, and land on exactly that frame, B-frames and long GOPs included, in MP4, Matroska and HLS. A plain MPEG-TS file has no index to seek by: a seek in one lands on the first keyframe after the frame asked for. SMPTE timecode is read from the file, drop-frame included; play, pause, an A–B loop, and J-K-L shuttle, forward only, from a quarter speed to eight times, with sound kept at its own pitch from half speed to double.
  • Loudness by EBU R128. Momentary, short-term and integrated loudness and true peak of what is heard, each channel weighted from the stream’s own layout, and a separate measurement of the whole file from start to end.
  • The picture as decoded. Paused, the Y′CbCr and RGB values of the pixel under the cursor; and any frame saved as a PNG. Each frame is drawn with the colour matrix its file declares; BT.2020 and P3 standard-dynamic-range pictures are converted to BT.709’s gamut, and the stream panel says when they were.
  • What the file says against what it does. Codec, size, pixel format, colour tags and frame rate, with the frame rate the file declares checked against the one it actually runs at. For MXF, what the wrapper says (its operational pattern, field order and audio track map) is read and shown, and its field order is checked against the picture inside it.
  • Sound held to its picture, with a trim to move it earlier or later, and a readout of how closely it is following. The readout measures the player’s own tracking; it cannot see the delay a display or a headset adds, which is why we measure that on the screen (below).
  • Output to a chosen screen. The picture can be sent full screen to a named display. The main display is left out of that list, so the player cannot cover the screen you are working on unless you ask it to.
  • Hardware decoding where the graphics hardware offers it (VA-API on Linux, D3D11VA on Windows), with software decoding where it does not. Hardware-decoded frames are drawn without being copied back to the processor: on Linux with Intel graphics, and on Windows since 26 Oct 2026, where each frame is copied once within the graphics processor.

What it plays

As of 29 Oct 2026. Every codec below is compiled into our build and checked to be there; H.264, AV1, MPEG-2, ProRes, DNxHD and DNxHR, v210, MPEG-4 Part 2, VP8, AAC, Opus and PCM are also played by tests of ours.

Containers MP4 and MOV, Matroska and WebM, MXF, MPEG-TS; HLS streams, on demand and live
Video H.264, VP9 and AV1; MPEG-2 (its 4:2:2 profile included), ProRes 422, DNxHD and DNxHR, and v210; MPEG-4 Part 2, H.263 and VP8
Pictures shown Planar 4:2:0 and 4:2:2 at 8 and 10 bits, and packed 8-bit 4:2:2 (uncompressed 2vuy), limited or full range. Planar 4:2:2 since 25 Oct 2026, packed since 26 Oct 2026; drawn as it is coded, each line with its own colour
Audio AAC, MP3, FLAC, Opus, Vorbis and PCM, in a file that also has a picture

Its media libraries are FFmpeg’s, built by us from FFmpeg’s own source under the GNU Lesser General Public License (LGPL) version 2.1, with nothing licensed under the GPL inside them. Their source and the script that built them will be published beside each download. Why we build them ourselves is in the archive: why we build our own FFmpeg.

Where it is tested

As of 25 Oct 2026, on two machines, both with Intel graphics. Until 7 Oct 2026 it was developed on a laptop with AMD Radeon Vega graphics under Linux; nothing since, the path that draws hardware-decoded frames in place included, has run on AMD graphics.

  • A laptop, a Lenovo ThinkPad E16 Gen 3 with an Intel Core Ultra 7 255H, under Ubuntu 24.04 LTS: where it is developed, and where every change is tested.
  • A mini PC, a GMKtec NucBox G3 with an Intel N100, under Windows 11 and under Ubuntu 24.04 LTS, on an ordinary monitor and USB headsets: where the Windows build is tested, and where the sound and picture above were measured.

What we don’t claim

As of 29 Oct 2026
  • Not for compliance use. What the player shows, the loudness and the timecode included, is for information. It is not a broadcast monitoring, measurement or compliance instrument.
  • Tested on Intel graphics only. As of 25 Oct 2026 the player is tested on Intel graphics. It ran on AMD Radeon Vega graphics under Linux until 7 Oct 2026, and not since; it has never run on NVIDIA graphics, and on Linux it has no NVIDIA decoding path.
  • On 24 Oct 2026 the sound reached the listener 10 ms (Ubuntu) and 22 ms (Windows) after its picture on the set-up we measured, by latency that no device reported to us. The Windows build has changed how it draws since then and has not been measured again. We have not corrected for it, and we have measured one display and one headset.
  • Seeking in MXF is not covered by our frame-accuracy test.
  • Not every picture is shown. 4:4:4 (ProRes 4444, DNxHR 444), 12-bit, RGB, and semi-planar 4:2:2 pictures (NV16, P210) are not drawn: the file plays, with its sound, timecode and loudness, and the picture area names the format it could not show. HDR (PQ and HLG) is not tone mapped, and the stream panel says so. Interlaced video is shown as it is coded, both fields in one frame, not deinterlaced.
  • The Windows program is not code-signed. Windows’ Smart App Control judges an unsigned program file by file, and on our mini PC it refused one of our test programs and allowed it four minutes later.
  • Not shown to survive a graphics driver reset. Since 29 Oct 2026 the build we test draws its picture again after its graphics device is lost and plays on, which we have tried only by destroying the device on purpose. A real driver reset has not been tried, and the hardware decoder is not rebuilt after one.
  • No macOS, and on Linux the native Wayland window is not measured: every measurement on Linux has been taken through X11 on Xwayland.
  • Computer displays only. The picture goes to the computer’s own displays: there is no SDI or IP output, and captions and AFD are not shown.
